2. Geometry, solder mask and exposed metal

Both sides drawn from the gerbers, rotated so the beam runs left to right with Heater 1 on the left. Solder mask covers all copper and all FR4 except the openings shown in silver. The heater and beam copper are fully mask-covered and every via is tented, so these surfaces radiate like the mask (ε ≈ 0.9), not like bare metal.

3. Cross-section through the heater and beam start

A section along the beam axis through the left resistor column (x = 7.62 mm). The sensor sits at x = 10.16 mm and is projected into the section. The vertical scale is about 2× horizontal so thin layers stay visible; copper and mask are drawn thicker still.

conduction path convection + radiation to air to the frame (parasitic)

Read the section bottom-up. The resistive thick film is on the chip's upper face, which faces away from the PCB once the part sits on the bottom side. Film heat therefore crosses the alumina body (k ≈ 24 W/m·K) sideways to the two wrap-around terminations, goes through the solder joints into the bottom copper pad, then up through the vias (and, far less, the FR4) into the top copper. On top it reaches Beam 1, then spreads along both copper layers of the beam. Every mask-covered face loses heat to the air; the exposed resistor body does too.

4. Thermal network

Lumped resistances in K/W at the operating point. Conductive links are fixed by geometry; surface links depend on temperature through hc(ΔT) and hr(T). The beam is a distributed fin (§7), drawn here as its input resistance.
